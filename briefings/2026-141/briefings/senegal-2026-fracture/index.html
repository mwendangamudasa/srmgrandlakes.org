Senegal: Fracture at the Top

23 May 2026 · SRM Analysis · SRM Research Team

The dismissal of Sonko by Faye fractures the PASTEF project and reconfigures the West African geopolitical board, in a Sahel where terrorism is intensifying and Françafrique is attempting a return via the flanks.

Four Lines of Fracture

The regional political and security context in four dimensions · 23 May 2026

01 · Senegal · Internal Politics

Sonko Dismissed, PASTEF Government Dissolved

On 22 May 2026, Faye signed the decree ending Sonko's functions. The founding alliance of the Senegalese rupture shattered after 26 months. Sonko was perceived as seeking to appropriate the presidential vision to prepare his own trajectory.

02 · Sahel · Regional Security

JNIM–FLA: Coordinated Attacks, Kidal Taken, AES Weakened

On 25–26 April 2026, the JNIM+FLA coalition took Kidal. Russian mercenaries were forced into partial withdrawal. Between January 2025 and April 2026: 3,291 civilians killed in 561 attacks across the AES countries (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger).

03 · Chad · Lake Chad

Boko Haram Kills 23 Soldiers, National Mourning Declared

4 and 6 May 2026: double attack on the Barka Tolorom base. 23 Chadian soldiers killed. Mahamat Deby decreed 20 days of national mourning. ISWAP and Boko Haram in violent competition for control of the lake basin.

04 · Geopolitics · Françafrique

France Attempts a Return via Three Coastal Flanks

Expelled from Mali (2022), Burkina Faso (2023) and Niger (2023), France is consolidating its residual positions in Benin and Côte d'Ivoire, and watching post-Sonko Senegal's positioning as a possible strategic reinsertion.

Faye–Sonko: Chronology of a Rupture

From the founding alliance to the brutal dismissal · 22–23 May 2026

March 2024 — PASTEF Victory
Faye elected in the first round. Sonko named Prime Minister. The duo embodied the anti-neocolonial, pan-African rupture. Massive popular support, notably among urban youth.
Jul–Nov 2025 — Rising Tensions
Sonko accused Faye of a "lack of authority". Public criticism of the "last-minute allies". Disagreements over debt, the IMF, and the nomination of Aminata Touré to head the coalition. The economic line began to diverge.
March 2026 — Implicit Ultimatum
Sonko publicly declared he could withdraw PASTEF from government if Faye was no longer "aligned" with the party's radical vision. The party closed ranks around Sonko against the presidential coalition.
22 May 2026 — Dismissal Decree
Decree 2026-1128: end of Sonko's functions and dissolution of the government. Announced on RTS. No new Prime Minister named. Sonko supporters took to the streets of Dakar. Sonko posted on Facebook: "Tonight I will sleep peacefully."
SRM Strategic Reading

Sonko's dismissal does not mean the end of Sonkoism: it potentially frees Sonko from the institutional framework and confers on him the status of political martyr. His base, essentially young and urban, is a vector of social instability to watch closely. A return to Françafrique networks remains a plausible hypothesis if Faye seeks rapid international diplomatic recognition.

Senegalese Repositioning

Implications for neighbouring countries and for the AES–ECOWAS dynamic

Senegal

A Post-Breakup Pivot Towards Paris?

Sonko was the anti-Françafrique lock. His departure raises the question of Faye's diplomatic pragmatism. Faye's planned visit to the Senegal–France World Cup match (16 June) takes on a new symbolic dimension. Western chancelleries are watching.

Risk: Faye forced to show his diplomatic independence so as not to appear as a return to the old order.

AES (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger)

Increased Distrust Towards Dakar

The Sahelian juntas had seen the Faye–Sonko duo as a potential ally. The dismissal of Sonko, a radical pan-Africanist figure, is perceived in Bamako, Ouagadougou and Niamey as a negative signal and a possible reintegration of Dakar into the ECOWAS–France orbit.

Risk: Diplomatic isolation of Senegal in the sovereigntist sub-region. Activation of indirect destabilization channels.

Benin & Ivory Coast

Reinforced Pro-France Atlantic Front

If Senegal joins Benin and Ivory Coast in a pro-Western stance, France will re-establish an Atlantic belt: Dakar–Abidjan–Cotonou. This strategic corridor provides Paris with a logistical, diplomatic, and economic base to contain the influence of the AES and Russia westward.

Risk: AES countries perceive this arc as a threat of encirclement, potentially a catalyst for terrorist attacks on coastal countries.

ECOWAS Dynamics

The AES–ECOWAS divide is deepening. Senegal, long ambiguous, could now lean towards the ECOWAS–France camp, weakening any regional mediation and radicalizing the positioning of the Sahel juntas.

The central Sahel, particularly the Mali–Burkina–Niger tripoint, has become the epicentre of global jihadist violence. The JNIM–FLA coalition (April 2026) demonstrated unprecedented operational capacity: the capture of Kidal, the partial withdrawal of Russian forces. On 16 May 2026, a joint USA–Nigeria strike eliminated Abu-Bilal al-Minuki, the global number two of Islamic State, in the Lake Chad basin, a signal that the region has become a global strategic node of jihadism.

In Chad, the attacks of 4 and 6 May 2026 on the Barka Tolorom base killed 23 soldiers. Deby decreed 20 days of mourning. ISWAP and Boko Haram are fighting for control of the lake's islands and trade corridors, a territorial war that feeds regional instability.

Critical trend: jihadist groups are adapting their strategy: tactical alliances, use of drones and IEDs, night attacks. The absence of coherent regional cooperation between AES and ECOWAS creates a security vacuum these groups systematically exploit.

The Pro-France Atlantic Arc

After its military and diplomatic expulsion from Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger between 2022 and 2023, France concentrated its presence on the coastal countries. A potential post-Sonko Senegalese tilt would complete a strategic encirclement belt around the AES countries, perceived by them as an indirect destabilization strategy.

Senegal

Pivot in Suspense

Post-Sonko, Faye is awaited on his posture towards Paris. Historic French military presence in Dakar (naval base). Senegal is the northern Atlantic façade, the gateway to the western Sahel. Critical tilt under way.

Ivory Coast

Consolidated French Anchor

Ouattara remains Paris's strategic ally in West Africa. Ivory Coast hosts 900 French soldiers (4th BIMA). Northern border with Burkina Faso exposed to jihadist infiltration, JNIM attacks documented in northern Ivory Coast since 2021.

Benin

Eastern Front under Pressure

Benin faces growing jihadist incursions in the north (W–Arly–Pendjari park). Paris maintains active security cooperation. Cotonou is perceived as the south-eastern lock of the French arrangement in the region.

SRM Hypothesis

If Senegal tilts towards a pro-French posture, the AES countries will perceive the Dakar–Abidjan–Cotonou arc as an existential threat, likely to accelerate tacit or explicit support for destabilization movements in these three coastal countries, via jihadist proxies or internal political agitation.
